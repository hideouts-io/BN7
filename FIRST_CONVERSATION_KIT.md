## Conversation sheet

**What BridgeNode7 does.** BridgeNode7 develops methods and reference software that connect technical decisions with supporting evidence, assumptions, and human review. The [public playbook](PUBLIC_PLAYBOOK.md#how-the-work-connects) explains how those relationships help identify reasoning that needs another review when conditions change. Qualified people remain responsible for technical judgments and decisions.

**Audience hypothesis.** Technical leads at consultancies that review AI or cybersecurity systems. Their own review workflow is the starting point. A consultancy might be a prospective user, collaborator, referral partner, or peer; its service category alone does not establish purchasing interest. The existing [AI/cyber partner guide](https://github.com/Bridge-Node-7/ai-cyber-assurance/blob/9033bf73bbd3923c5de0a9c6fb9959b4c140de64/13-assurance-intelligence/partner-kit/START_HERE.md) supports the workflow to discuss, not the market selection.

**Problem hypothesis.** After a system, evidence, or assumption changes, reviewers may need to reconstruct which earlier conclusions still apply. Whether that creates meaningful difficulty, and whether their current tools already handle it well, must be established through actual examples.

**Proposed next step.** A 20-minute discovery conversation about one recent review: the decision, its owner, any relevant change, the steps taken, and what worked or did not work. Start with a non-confidential overview, consistent with the [published partner route](https://bridgenode7.com/partner/). The time is a proposed interview length, not an assessment-delivery promise.

**Useful result.** An accurate account of a relevant workflow, including a documented difficulty, a clear lack of fit, or a question worth investigating next. If further technical scoping would be useful, first establish an accountable owner, appropriate evidence access, and feasible BN7 participation. The [documented evaluation approach](https://github.com/Bridge-Node-7/frontier-mission-assurance/blob/f99dd2174c74f26b648321e02f8d30e2cff10230/docs/USE_AND_EVALUATION.md#governed-one-decision-evaluation) does not establish an available commercial package.

**Existing material.** The playbook links public worked examples; its changed-assumption example is synthetic. Keep examples separate from customer proof. The [evidence register](research/EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md#c20--business-evidence-remains-unestablished) records the remaining business-evidence gaps.

## Spoken introduction

This 66-word draft takes approximately 26–33 seconds at 120–150 words per minute. That is a calculated estimate; your actual delivery has not been timed.

> I work in business development with BridgeNode7. We develop methods and reference software that connect technical decisions with their supporting evidence, assumptions, and human review. I'm exploring how AI and cybersecurity reviewers handle changes that could affect earlier conclusions. I'd like to understand one recent example from your work, including what already works well and where, if anywhere, you had difficulty. Please keep the overview non-confidential.

## Twenty minute discovery script

Use this script for preparation now. An actual conversation remains a later human action. Ask about work the person actually did before discussing possible solutions. Preserve answers that contradict the problem hypothesis. Use the main question at each stage and follow up only where useful; the prompts are not a checklist to exhaust in twenty minutes.

| Minutes | Purpose | Specific prompts and notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–2 | Establish context. | Use the introduction. Ask, “What is your role in technical reviews?” Record the person's stated role. |
| 2–7 | Obtain a recent example. | “What is the most recent review where you revisited an earlier conclusion? What decision was being made, and who owned it?” If no example exists, record that and ask about the most recent ordinary review; do not invent a change event. |
| 7–11 | Reconstruct the actual process. | Main question for either path: “Walk me through the steps, tools, and evidence you used.” If a change was reported, ask how they determined which earlier conclusions still applied. Optional follow-up: “How do you keep the original review record while assessing the changed situation?” Distinguish the described process from your interpretation. |
| 11–15 | Understand consequences. | “Where, if anywhere, did the process create repeated work, delays, or uncertainty? What happened as a result?” Label any time or effort estimate as the practitioner's estimate. |
| 15–18 | Challenge the hypothesis. | “What already works well? Can you describe a similar review that was easy to handle?” If a difficulty was reported: “Is this important enough to improve, or are other problems more pressing?” Record effective existing solutions, no difficulty, and low priority as useful findings. |
| 18–20 | Confirm understanding. | “My understanding is [specific situation]. What have I missed or overstated?” Record whether another question or discussion would be useful. An explicit lack of fit is a valid result. |

If they ask about a price, delivery date, or packaged service, use: “I am investigating the workflow first. I cannot quote a price or delivery commitment at this stage. If there is a useful fit, we can identify who should scope the next discussion.” Record the request under the deferred questions in the business development brief.

Copy this template for a real conversation when one occurs. Leave unasked or unanswered fields as “Not assessed”; do not turn blanks into evidence of absence. Keep direct statements distinct from your assessment.

```text

Conversation date: Not assessed
Recent review, decision, and accountable role: Not assessed
Changed condition, or no change example reported: Not assessed
Actual steps and tools described: Not assessed
What already works well: Not assessed
Difficulty reported, or explicit absence of difficulty: Not assessed
Consequence and self-reported effort estimate: Not assessed
Priority relative to other work: Not assessed
Counterexample or existing solution: Not assessed
Exact relevant statements: Not assessed
My interpretation and evidence supporting it: Not assessed
Fit assessment and reason: Not assessed

```

One person's experience establishes information about that person's situation. It does not establish broad demand, a qualified buyer, or a BN7 customer outcome.

The [organization research](research/BD_CANDIDATES.md) found public descriptions of existing reassessment, versioning, evidence records, or retesting. For such organizations, use a peer-learning framing: “Your published approach already addresses this workflow. I would like to understand what it handles well and whether any boundary remains unresolved.” This is an optional conversation framing, not another spoken introduction or a claim of commercial complementarity.

## Account research criteria

The [ten-organization research list](research/BD_CANDIDATES.md) now applies these criteria under the audience hypothesis. Continue using official service pages, methodologies, published reports, or team pages for further research. Separate what the source says from why you think it could matter to BN7.

| Criterion | Evidence to record | What remains unknown |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relevant activity | An official source showing AI/cyber assessment, assurance review, or evaluation work. | Whether that organization needs a different approach. |
| Specific workflow | A described review, evidence process, findings report, retest, or reassessment method. | Whether maintaining the decision basis creates difficulty. |
| A useful conversation topic | One concrete activity or published initiative that supports a specific discovery question. Record its publication date when available and your review date. | The organization's present urgency or priorities. |
| Relevant role | A publicly documented role responsible for the work, or an explicitly labeled role hypothesis to investigate. | Purchasing authority and willingness to speak. |
| Existing approach and overlap | What the organization describes: evidence history, dataset/system versioning, thresholds, dependencies, original review records, change triggers, or retesting. Undescribed handling is “Not established,” not absent. | Whether BN7 would complement it, duplicate it, or offer no useful fit; described capability is not independently tested performance. |
| Relationship hypothesis | Possible workflow user, collaborator, referral partner, delivery partner, peer, or unresolved. | Actual relationship interest and commercial potential. |
| Existing connection | A relationship supported by information actually supplied to you; otherwise record “Not established.” | Access to the person and permission for outreach. |

**Ready for the research list** means the organization has an official identity/source, documented relevant activity, a source-backed conversation topic, a role or labeled role hypothesis, an explicit fit hypothesis, and a question that could disprove it. This is research completeness, not lead qualification.

**More evidence needed** means those fields cannot yet be supported. **Outside the current hypothesis** means the reviewed activity does not match the proposed audience. Record the reason rather than inventing a numerical score. Treat organizations with substantial overlapping products as peers or possible collaborators until a specific complementary need is established.

Use this record for each researched organization:

```text

```

A researched organization is a candidate. Actual need, budget, timing, buyer authority, and technical fit require further evidence. A company appearing in BN7's public research is not thereby a BN7 partner or customer.
