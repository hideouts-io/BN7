# Bridge Node 7: longer playbook outline

**Expansion plan only · October 4, 2026 · Short edition first**

The [short edition](PUBLIC_PLAYBOOK.md) is the first review deliverable. This outline defines a deeper edition without beginning a second full manuscript before the short version's direction has been reviewed. Execution and publication tasks belong in [TODO.md](TODO.md).

## Proposed form and reader promise

Target approximately **5,000–8,000 words**, excluding a compact source index, organized as an accessible web document with an optional PDF generated from the same manuscript. Length is a proposal to confirm after short-edition review. Readers should be able to move from an understandable decision question to the underlying records, methods, implementation, and limits.

The central narrative and factual claims should agree with the short edition. The longer edition adds explanations, evidence, and domain depth; it should link canonical specifications rather than reproduce them. The shared [evidence register](research/EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md) owns the claim-to-source map.

## 1. Why the decision basis matters

**Reader question:** why can a reasonable decision deserve another review?

Expand the opening with a clearly fictional decision that relies on an experiment, an estimate, and a supplier assertion. Explain changes in applicability, evidence quality, uncertainty, and time. Compare the decision-time view with the later changed view while preserving the original record.

**Sources:** profile lifecycle and Decision Surface; FMA Mission Decision Packet. **Visual:** one decision with its supporting inputs. **Needed:** approved narrative and audience emphasis; no quantified benefits without measured evidence.

## 2. From a claim to an evidence record

**Reader question:** what can a source actually establish?

Explain reported information, observation, measurement, calculation, inference, simulation, hypothesis, reproduction, review, and decision. Use FIW's fictional three-reports/one-root example to show why repetition is not independent corroboration. Introduce countercases, discriminating evidence, contradictory records, and reassessment.

**Sources:** FIW source genealogy, perception/language integrity, limitations, and fictional diligence. **Visual:** three derivative reports feeding one underlying source. **Needed:** qualified methodology review; external scholarly comparisons only after primary-source research.

## 3. Architecture and responsibility

**Reader question:** which part of BN7 does what?

Describe public research methods, domain records, assurance relationships, decision preparation, and accountable human judgment. Explain the difference between an architectural role, a public implementation, and observed integration. Identify Intelligence Library and Mission Graph only within the scope their public documents establish; do not imply public deployments or availability.

**Sources:** architecture, lifecycle, Decision Surface, execution authority. **Visual:** responsibility map with explicit source ownership. **Needed:** review of public/private naming and supported integration claims.

## 4. FMA in detail

**Reader question:** how does a changed input affect a declared decision basis?

Explain graphs, evidence coverage, assumptions, dependency impact, research receipts, decision receipts, and reopen conditions. Distinguish file identity from fresh reproduction and reproduction from scientific validity. Follow the synthetic movement-loss example from its initial HOLD through changed conditions, stale estimates, applicability limits, and review flagged for reopening.

**Sources:** assurance-study.md and its pinned implementation/tests/contracts. **Visuals:** initial vs changed state; receipt/reproduction distinctions. **Evaluation evidence now available:** the private FMA validator/comparison run at the exact inspected commit reproduces the synthetic HOLD and change-impact signals. Preserve provenance warnings, comparison-derived staleness, and source-versus-release identity. **Needed:** named domain review and any deeper receipt/estimator evaluation required by the expanded chapter.

## 5. Materials and industrial pathways

**Reader question:** where does evidence stop between a material and a usable capability?

Explain Material Assurance Records, derived Decision Passports, unknowns, evidence obligations, and qualification gaps. Contrast the synthetic missing-origin example with the reviewed public-source Gallium pathway. Introduce YIG only with its research-context limitations. Use Pax Silica to explain the evidence needed between an announcement and repeatable qualified output.

**Sources:** decision-materials-study.md; supporting-study.md; Atlas dated snapshot. **Visual:** supported links, first unresolved link, later context. **Needed:** current primary sources for any fresh industrial or supply-chain claim; preserve M0 maturity and dated-review boundaries.

## 6. Decision preparation and accountable choice

**Reader question:** what does a comparison calculate, and what does a person still decide?

Explain FDE's working brief, explicit choices/objectives/scenarios, desirability assumptions, threshold comparison, insufficient-information/tie states, human attestation, decision receipts, and reassessment deltas. Give a small public-safe example whose inputs are visibly illustrative. Explain data handling for the public browser deployment.

**Sources:** FDE architecture, methodology, calculation implementation, receipt schema, pilot gates. **Visual:** frame → compare → human decision → receipt. **Needed:** selected example and bounded browser/calculation validation; no fabricated direct integration with M2M or other components.

## 7. Domain chapters selected by reader need

Choose depth after reviewing the short edition. Candidate sections:

- **Scientific discovery:** specification, replication, provenance, proof state, and human obligations.
- **AI and cybersecurity:** permitted system/agent authority, linked controls, corrective actions, and retests using ACA's synthetic case.
- **Space cryptographic transition:** inventories, exposure, ten readiness domains, evidence confidence, critical overrides, migration/recovery, and a human decision pack.
- **Orbital recovery:** separate physical state, trust, authority, options, and requalification; synthetic benchmark boundaries.
- **Extreme uncertainty:** source genealogy, competing explanations, prohibited inferences, and evidence capable of changing an assessment.

**Sources:** respective public profiles and studies. **Needed:** qualified reviewers and current primary references for domain-specific science, standards, or legal applicability claims. These chapters are educational applications, not assertions of customers or operational missions.

## 8. Academic and technical evaluation

**Reader question:** how can I inspect, reproduce, or challenge the method?

Provide reading paths, revision selection, citation guidance, prerequisites, expected and observed outputs, and limits. Describe research questions about source independence, changed applicability, reproducibility, human comprehension, and review effort. Use repository-native evaluation instructions through links; avoid a second maintained command manual.

**Sources:** exact-revision evaluations, tests, citation metadata, use terms, and validation contracts. **Available:** exact-command synthetic FMA evaluation records. **Needed:** further execution only for added claims, real reader/user-study evidence, and scholarly literature selected from primary sources. Test definitions and hosted workflow status retain their own evidence class.

## 9. Engagement, adoption questions, and institutional context

**Reader question:** what would a useful collaboration establish?

Explain the published assessment/engagement paths and the proposed one-decision pilot. Define inputs, responsibilities, acceptance, baseline measures, and review triggers. Present approved company identity and commercial facts only when supplied. Investors receive an entry to technical diligence and a clear route to separately controlled business evidence.

**Sources:** partner page, public pilot-readiness methods, approved company facts. **Needed:** actual delivery terms and outcome evidence before turning proposals into public offerings or traction claims.

## 10. Limits, sources, and maintenance

**Reader question:** what should I rely on, and how will corrections remain visible?

Explain evidence scope, mixed software rights, canonical sources, source vs release revisions, attribution, public handling, and correction ownership. Link the authoritative technical topics and exact reference editions. The public source index should stay compact; private research records and task lists stay private.

**Needed:** named editor, public publisher, correction route, and approved release process. The longer edition must receive its own source, reader, accessibility, and public-release review after expansion.
